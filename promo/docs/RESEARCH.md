Sources were read on 2026-10-03. "Read" = fetched and read in full or in the relevant part; quotes are paraphrased unless marked.

## 1. The reference: mexicat/pdoom-video (MIT)

Cloned to `/tmp/pdoom-video` (README, `docs/TREATMENT.md`, `docs/ENGINE.md`, `app/src/engine/{engine,post,gl,util,audio,scene}.ts`, `app/src/main.ts`, `app/src/timeline.ts`, `app/scripts/render.ts`, `analysis/analyze.py`, plate stills in `app/public/plates/`).

What makes it good, in its own words and in the code:

- **Every frame is a pure function of song time.** Preview and export share one code path; randomness is seeded (`mulberry32`, `hash`), per-frame jitter uses `frameIdx(t)` so a frame shows one state across its shutter. → Adopted as the first rule of `promo/` (see `NOTES.md` there).
- **The edit is data anchored to the music.** `timeline.ts` places scene windows by lyric lines and snaps them to the beat grid; scenes look times up instead of hard-coding them. → Our plates are declared in bars in `data/timeline.json`; bars resolve through the analysed downbeats in `data/audio.json`.
- **Adaptive motion blur.** Each exported frame averages 4 → 12 → 36 → 108 → 324 sub-frames (ternary offsets, every step centred on the frame time) until another step would move the displayed image by less than `--tol` 8-bit levels (worst 2×2 px block). Still frames stop at 12, whips go to 108/324. → Engine ported (MIT notice in `THIRD_PARTY.md`).
- **Post is restrained and physical.** HDR linear compositing, a Jimenez 13-tap bloom pyramid with soft knee, red-orange halation from a low mip, a tone *shoulder* (identity below 0.72), two-scale film grain weighted to mid-tones, 1/255 dither, BT.709-tagged x264 (`-tune grain`, CRF 16, AAC 320k). "Only signal/ember should exceed ~0.85 linear (i.e. glow). Bone type must stay crisp, never blooming." → Same chain; for pixel art we drop chromatic aberration and keep grain low so sprite edges stay single-pixel sharp.
- **Style bible discipline.** One ink/bone palette plus one signal colour; one rare accent "owned by one moment" (acid, ~2 s); two to three type families with fixed roles; hard cuts on downbeats; "strong eases (outExpo, inOutCubic, springs), holds, then snaps"; "no lens-flare soup, no generic particle nebulae, no stock AI imagery". The plates (`app/public/plates/fig*.jpg`) show the result: dark field, one hot colour, hairlines, a single large typographic idea per frame, small mono annotations. → Our palette is the game's own UI (navy ink, bone text, gold signal); the six rarity colours are the "rare accent" and appear only in the rarity plate.
- **Process.** The concept, treatment, analysis, renderer and renders were done with Claude Opus 5.5 in Claude Code (README). The workflow that made it reliable: render stills/contact sheets, *look at them*, fix, repeat (`ENGINE.md` "Stills (the main way to check your work — then LOOK at the PNGs)").

## 2. Trailer craft (Derek Lieu, game-trailer editor)

- [10 Common Indie Game Trailer Mistakes](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2020/9/14/10-common-indie-game-trailer-mistakes-and-how-to-fix-them): no logo up front (logo on the end slate); no slow intro — show what the game looks like in the first shot(s); avoid repetition ("nothing will make your game look smaller"); good **eye trace** (keep the focus where the eye already is across cuts); **music must be structured for trailers** — "a good beginning, middle and end … build and build … to a final climax"; looping game BGM usually is not.
  → We did not reuse the game's looping BGM; we generated a dedicated cue with a real arc (intro → groove → breakdown → finale → cadence → final chord). Every plate keeps its subject on the frame's centre column, so cuts never make the eye jump.
- [Editing an Engaging Trailer Opening](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2019/10/2/editing-an-engaging-trailer-opening): the first ~6 seconds decide retention; a cold open must hook. → The first creature appears at bar 2 (≈5.3 s), not after a long title sequence.
- [Simple Game Trailer Templates](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2020/3/1/simple-game-trailer-templates): "tell, show, tell, show"; sequence simplest → most complex; "show the dream, not the job"; "retention of knowledge is far more important than quantity of content". → One idea per plate, each card names an outcome (meet them, collect 190, rarity by real capability, an endless world, friends), not a mechanic.
- [Game Trailer Makeover — Sheepherds](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2026/8/4/game-trailer-makeover-sheepherds) (2026-08): give viewers time to process each idea; cosmetics/variety only after the core is understood; title-card copy must be understood by people outside the team.
- [Dear Passengers vs Banana Airways](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2026/8/23/dear-passengers-vs-banana-airways) (2026-08): small polish details (a pat on the head) read as quality; the viewer should be able to infer the loop.

## 3. HD-2D presentation

- [Wikipedia: HD-2D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HD-2D): pixel art and billboard sprites in fully 3D environments; dynamic lighting, depth of field and **tilt-shift** produce a **diorama** look; coined by Square Enix for Octopath Traveler (2018, with Acquire), later Triangle Strategy, Live A Live, Dragon Quest remakes. Early prototypes put FF6 sprites under dramatic lighting.
- The game's own renderer notes (`docs/research/three-hd2d-notes.md`, read-only): low internal resolution + nearest upscale or the hybrid path (pixelated scene, smooth full-res bloom/DOF/vignette on top); screen-space tilt-shift is the recommended HD-2D DOF (no sprite halos, unlike depth-based bokeh with alpha-cut sprites); bloom only above ~1.0 linear (emissives); skip FXAA for pixel art.
  → The world plate: three.js r186, 32-px textures with nearest filtering, billboard sprites with real shadows, warm key light, fog, screen-space tilt-shift band that follows the subject, bloom only on emissives.

## 4. Pixel-art scaling (crispness is non-negotiable)

- [Cole Cecil, Scaling Pixel Art Without Destroying It](https://colececil.dev/blog/2017/scaling-pixel-art-without-destroying-it/), building on [A Personal Wonderland: manual texture filtering for pixelated games](https://csantosbh.wordpress.com/2014/01/25/manual-texture-filtering-for-pixelated-games-in-webgl/): integer scales are trivially crisp; at non-integer scales nearest makes uneven pixels and bilinear blurs; the fix is a shader that snaps UVs to texel centres and blends only across a one-screen-pixel band at texel edges.
  → All sprites (2D and billboards) use that filter (`fwidth`-based, linear sampler). At integer scale and pixel-aligned positions it equals nearest; while squashing, scaling or moving it stays sharp without shimmer. Holds use integer scales (1×, 2×, 3×, 4×) and pixel-aligned positions; text in the pixel face is rasterised at its 12-px design size and scaled by integers.
- [YouTube recommended upload encoding settings](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171?hl=en): MP4, fast start, H.264 High, 4:2:0, same frame rate as produced, AAC-LC 48 kHz stereo; 1080p60 SDR ≈ 12 Mbps recommended. → We export 1080p60 x264 CRF 16 (well above that), `+faststart`, BT.709 tags, AAC 48 kHz.

## 5. Takeaways that shape the treatment

1. One spine idea, said in very few words: you talk to AIs every day — here is what they look like — every one is a companion — in your pocket.
2. Hook within 6 s with a real asset; logo only at the end.
3. A music cue with a trailer arc; cuts on downbeats; accents on analysed onsets; a breath before the final chord.
4. Restraint: dark navy field, bone type, gold signal; the rarity colours are spent in one plate; no lens flares, no nebulae.
5. Pixel art shown big, centred, integer-scaled, never smeared; motion via whole-sprite translation, gentle squash on landings.
6. Distinct plates, simplest → richest (one face → 190 → rarity → world → friends); no repeated shot ideas.
7. Two typefaces: Fusion Pixel (the game's own face, OFL) for Chinese display and labels; IBM Plex Mono (OFL) for small English/metadata — the "machine" voice.

## 6. Revision 1 (2026-10-03): sound, load, the ending

Fetched with `omnireach fetch` (WebSearch unavailable); read in full.

- [Why Your Game Trailer Needs Sound Effects ON](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2020/1/10/why-your-game-trailer-needs-sound-effects-on): sound effects make actions tactile; the failure mode is SFX that are not integrated with the soundtrack ("the sound of text writing onto the screen is an invasive low buzzing sound"); treat SFX "as part of the trailer's musical score … an unusual percussion track", nudged onto the beat. → The game's own chiptune recipes, transposed to the cue's D major, every one on the cue that drives the picture; typing ticks are short, high and −29 dBFS.
- [Basic Guide to Trailer Sound Editing](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2020/11/24/basic-guide-to-trailer-sound-editing) and [Secrets to Trailer Sound Design](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2022/1/17/secrets-to-trailer-sound-design): one "hero audio" at a time, SFX in the music's troughs; the five jobs of trailer SFX (atmosphere, anticipation/risers, transitions, impact, sweetening). → A riser only where the picture builds (the MYTHIC charge: the game's `evolve`), impacts only on the two hits, the logo and MYTHIC; the breakdown (world) is left to the strings apart from soft landing pops.
- [Tension and Trailer Stopdowns](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2020/1/10/tension-and-trailer-stopdowns): a stop releases tension and says "this is gonna be big"; title cards land after the final stopdown. → The pocket gather is the stopdown (everything collapses into one point that rocks twice); the title lands on the catch sting.
- [Trailer Accents and Punctuation](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2019/5/15/trailer-accents-and-punctuation): accents delineate ideas and give breaths between them. → One accent per idea: the unpack, the 190 count, MYTHIC, the hit, the catch.
- [Considering Audience Cognitive Load](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2022/1/7/considering-audience-cognitive-load): "find ways to REMOVE as much information as possible"; per shot ask whether each element is needed, already covered, or distracting (HUD, characters, props, on-screen text, verbs). → Cut the trade beat, the ladder captions, the separate name tags and arrows in `together`, the battle panel's 14-px mono; the rarity line holds for 4 beats only.
- [How to Optimize a Game Trailer for Social Media](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2023/2/12/how-to-optimize-a-game-trailer-for-social-media): "you have mere microseconds to stop someone's thumb"; muted autoplay. → The cursor is on screen from frame 0 and typing by 0.5 s; every claim is also on screen as text, so the film reads muted.

## 7. Revision 2 (2026-10-03): grade, flashes, the missing verb

WebSearch was unavailable (quota exhausted) and the omnireach search bridge was not connected; pages were read with WebFetch / `omnireach fetch`. Not reachable: unrealengine.com (403, Octopath/HD-2D lighting talk), Wikipedia via curl.

- [W3C WCAG 2.2, SC 2.3.1 Three Flashes or Below Threshold](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html): a *general flash* is a pair of opposing luminance changes of ≥ 10 % where the darker state is below 0.80 relative luminance; it only counts if the flashing area exceeds 25 % of any 10° field of view (≈ 341 × 256 px at 1024 × 768); no more than three per second. → Full-frame lifts were the worst case. The MYTHIC and logo flashes are now radial on the subject with a 1-frame core and a 12 ms half-life (one flash each, corners +4 luma), and the absorb/caught flashes are small.
- [Scale2x — algorithm](https://www.scale2x.it/algorithm): EPX rules; each pixel becomes 2×2 using only its four neighbours' colours — no blur, no new colours, so the result is still pixel art. → Trainer sheets (64 px) go through Scale2x (with a colour tolerance of 24 for the sheets' antialiased edges) so their edge steps match the 128-px creatures.
- [Derek Lieu, Game Trailer Makeover – Dosa Divas](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2026/4/12/game-trailer-makeover-dosa-divas) (2026-04): show *why* you do things; "pretty places alone do not sell a game. Knowing what you do there will motivate people to WANT to go." → The `catch` plate: the genre's verb (find one in the grass, throw, shake, caught) before the rarity ladder and the world, and the caught one is the partner for the rest of the film, so the ending's two shakes + catch sting are a payoff, not a metaphor.
- [Derek Lieu, Professional Video Editing Tidbits](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2024/5/5/professional-video-editing-tidbits): work notes from the end backwards, keep every version, short audio fades, plan eye-trace across cuts. → The previous master is kept (`out/agent-pocket-promo.v2.mp4`); the catch push-in ends with the ball on the centre column where the rarity sprite lands next.
- The game's renderer notes (`docs/research/three-hd2d-notes.md`, read-only): tilt-shift → bloom → grade/vignette, in that order. → The diorama renders sky → scene → downsample → tilt-shift → grade, with bloom/vignette in post (`platePost`), and type is composited after the grade so bone copy is never dimmed.

## 8. Revision 3 (2026-10-03): HD-2D for real, golden hour

WebSearch was unavailable (quota) and the omnireach search bridge was not connected; pages were read with `omnireach fetch`.

- [David Holland, 3D Pixel Art Rendering](https://www.davidhol.land/articles/3d-pixel-art-rendering/) (2024-09, Godot, after t3ssel8r): render the 3D scene at a low internal resolution (his 640×360); 1-px outlines from a depth/normal edge kernel of the four neighbours only; snap the camera to a texel grid and shift the output back by the snap error; toon light; cloud shadows; volumetric god rays (light-space shells, depth-faded); particles drawn as single pixels. → The diorama's environment now renders at 1920/`look.pixelDiv` × 1080/`pixelDiv` (640×360) with a depth texture, is upscaled nearest-neighbour (texelFetch) into the 2× buffer with its depth, and gets a 1-px depth outline in that pass (faded with fog distance). Cloud shadows are value noise on the direct light; fireflies are one low-res pixel each (HDR, additive). Camera snapping was not added: the world camera is a slow crane at ≈ 1–3 low-res px per frame, and the motion blur already integrates the creep.
- [GPU Gems 3, ch. 13: Volumetric Light Scattering as a Post-Process](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-ii-light-and-shadows/chapter-13-volumetric-light-scattering-post-process) (Kenny Mitchell, EA): radial blur of an occlusion mask toward the light's screen position with density/decay/weight/exposure, added over the frame. → The god-ray pass masks sky pixels (depth = 1) weighted toward the sun disc, blurs them radially (48 samples, decay 0.955) and adds them before the downsample; skipped when the sun is behind the camera. Parameters: `world.json look.godRays`.
- The game's renderer notes (`docs/research/three-hd2d-notes.md`, read-only): §4 option B, the hybrid "true HD-2D" path (pixelated scene, full-res post on top); §0 `PCFSoftShadowMap` removed in r186; §9 `light.shadow.intensity` for soft (non-black) shadows. → Billboards render on camera layer 1 at full resolution against the upscaled depth (a small depth bias toward the camera keeps their feet on the tiles); shadows are PCF radius 3 at intensity 0.6, so their colour is the lavender hemisphere fill, not black.
- The game's own title key art (`public/assets/ui/title.png`) as the colour target — not an external source: a low sun on the horizon behind the subjects, violet zenith, orange horizon, warm rim light on the heroines. → Golden-hour key from front-left with a separate sky-sun direction behind the subjects (the sun is in frame, the faces stay lit), an inside-edge 1-art-px rim on the sun side of every billboard (`look.rim`), blob contact shadows (`look.blob`), emissive crystals, and the same palette in the sky shader so the world plate and the end slate belong together. (The Octopath/UE4 lighting talk was still not reachable; the HD-2D choices here are our reading of the sources above.)
